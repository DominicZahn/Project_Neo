\subsection{Rigid Body Dynamics} \label{sec:fundamentals:rigid}
The following equations are taken from Featherstones 2008 "Rigid Body Dynamics Algorithms" Book.
These are also the fundamentals for the algorithms implemented in for example Pinocchio \cite{pinocchioweb,carpentier2019pinocchio} and \acrfull{rbdl} \cite{Felis2016}.
\\
In its book Featherstone describes rigid body systems as collection of bodies connected by joints.
Each body is considered rigid so not able to change its shape and inertia like soft bodies would.
The whole rigid body system may also stand under the influence of various forces, while the joints restrict the resulting motions of the bodies.

\paragraph{Equations of Motions}
When a single rigid body $B$ is experiencing the force $\bm f$ its motion is described by
\begin{equation}
    \bm f = M \bm a + \bm p \label{eq:rbd:motionEq:rigid},
\end{equation}
where the inertia $M$ and bias force $\bm p$ are characteristics of the body and $a$ the resulting acceleration.
For an articulated body of multiple rigid bodies the equation is analogue to the one of a single body:
\begin{equation}
    \bm f = M^A \bm a + \bm p^A \label{eq:rbd:motionEq:articulated}
\end{equation}
The difference lies only in the inertia $I^A$ and bias force $\bm p^A$, which now also include the ones from $B'$.
Through usage of generalized forces $\tau$ instead of $\bm f$, as well as adding joint angle dependency, we specialize \cref{eq:rbd:motionEq:articulated} to the classical formulation of the equation of motions:
\begin{equation}
  \bm \tau = M(\bm q)\bm{\ddot q}+C(\bm q, \bm{\dot q}) \label{eq:rbd:motionEq:general}
\end{equation}
In \cref{eq:rbd:motionEq:general} $\bm q$ represents the joint angles, with joint velocities $\bm{\dot q}$ and accelerations $\bm{\ddot q}$.
$M(\bm q)$ denotes as the generalized inertia matrix of the rigid body system.
As the relative positioning of the rigid bodies towards each other changes the overall inertia of the system, it depends on the joint angle positions $\bm q$.
The generalized bias force is renamed to $C(\bm q, \bm{\dot q})$ a $\mathbb{R}^{n_q \cross n_q}$ matrix, to better fit its representation for Coriolis and centrifugal forces, gravity, and any other forces acting on the system that are not coming from the generalized force vector $\bm \tau$.
Because of that the generalized bias force is also a function of the joint angles $\bm q$ and velocities $\bm{\dot q}$.
\\
This results in \cref{eq:rbd:motionEq:general} being constructed from the variables $\bm q$ and $\bm \tau$, as well as the coefficients $M$ and $C$.

\paragraph{Forward Dynamics and Inverse Dynamics}
Based on previously formulated generalized equation of motion we are able to calculate either the generalized forces $\bm \tau$ or joint velocities $\bm{\dot q}$.

\begin{subequations}
  \begin{align}
    \bm{\ddot q} &= \text{FD}(\text{model},\bm q,\bm{\dot q},\bm \tau) \\
    \bm \tau &= \text{ID}(\text{model},\bm q,\bm{\dot q},\bm{\ddot q})
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

\paragraph{Constrained Dynamics}
Eq 2 in \cite{carpentier_2021}:
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:consDyn:default}
  \begin{align}
    \min_{\bm {\dot v}(\cdot)} \quad & \frac 1 2 \| \bm{\dot v} - \bm{\dot v_\text{free}}(\bm q, \bm v, \bm \tau) \|^2_M \label{eq:consDyn:default:obj} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & f_c(\bm q) = 0 \label{eq:consDyn:default:cons}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

Eq 3b in \cite{carpentier_2021}:
\begin{equation}
  J_{f_c}(\bm{q})\,\dot{\bm{v}} + \underbrace{\dot{J}_{f_c}(\bm{q},\bm{v})\,\bm{v}}_{\gamma_{f_c}(\bm{q},\bm{v})} = 0
  \label{eq:consDyn:default:jakobian}
\end{equation}

Eq 6 in \cite{carpentier_2021} (KKT Matrix):
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
  \mathbf{0} & J_{f_c} \\
  J_{f_c}^{t} & M
  \end{bmatrix}}_{K(\boldsymbol{q})}
  \begin{bmatrix}
  \boldsymbol{\lambda} \\
  \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}
  \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
  -\gamma_{f_c} \\
  M \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{free}}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \label{eq:consDyn:default:KKT}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Ill-conditioned Jakobian $J_{f_c}$}
"Significantly ill-conditioned" \\
Eq 9 Proximal Operator:
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{prox}_{f,\alpha}(y) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \arg\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \; f(x) + \frac{1}{2\alpha} \|x - y\|_2^2
  \label{eq:consDyn:prox:operator}
\end{equation}

Proximal Algorithms \cite{parikh_2014} with Iteration:
\begin{equation}
  x_{k+1} = \operatorname{prox}_{f,\alpha}(x_k)
  \label{eq:consDyn:prox:algo}
\end{equation}


Eq 18 in \cite{carpentier_2021} (KKT Matrix with proximal operator applied):
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
  \alpha I_m & J_{f_c} \\
  J_{f_c}^{t} & M
  \end{bmatrix}}_{K(\boldsymbol{q})}
  \begin{bmatrix}
  \boldsymbol{\lambda} \\
  \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}
  \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
  -\gamma_{f_c}- \alpha I_m \\
  M \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{free}}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \label{eq:consDyn:prox:KKT}
\end{equation}
