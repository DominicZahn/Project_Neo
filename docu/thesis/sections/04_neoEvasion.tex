\section{Neo} \label{sec:neo}
In the last section we identified a major shortcoming of our \textit{neo} method.
As the dodge constraint \cref{eq:neo:constraints:dodge} limits solely the last pose of the motion, the approaching object could collide in the time before with the robot, for example during a swing up torso move.
\\
Another problem pose approaching object with a stepper angle.
Those objects can not be avoided by robot ducking underneath a certain height, as the exact end pose and positioning of the body is important.
\\
Using the exact distance between object and robot body also enable the solver to find minimal evasion motions, where the robot perturbs away from the object, without the need for spacious and unstable moves.
The next step is therefore to improve our previous formulation, to form a descendant called \textit{Neo}.
\par
At first we are tackling the challenge of computing the distance between robot and approaching object (\cref{sec:neo:distance}).
Afterwards the formulation is adjusted to better suit the changes made in the dodge constraint.
Like it was done for \textit{neo} we also conclude the \textit{Neo} method by analyzing a specific motion, before a full testing of the method is done in \cref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Robot Object Distance} \label{sec:neo:distance}
Distance functions are an important tool in robotics and computer graphics to calculate object-to-object distances.
These functions define, for each point in space, the distance to the specified object \cite{jones_2006}.
\par
\todo[inline]{related work distance calculation}
\par
The difference to a \textit{signed} distance function is that it is negative inside the shape, while retaining positive values on the outside.
An inverse convention can also be found in some literature.
A \acrshort{sdf} functions easy to evaluate and compose properties show themselves when used in path-planning for robotic arms \cite{zhu_2025}, autonomous navigation of \acrshort{uav}s \cite{jacquet_2025} or dexterous manipulation \cite{yang_2025}. \\
Our work leverages a \acrshort{sdf} for the collision hull of our humanoid robot, it provides the distance between the robot and an approaching object $d(\bm{p}(t))$ for every point in time $t$.
The following section presents the collision model for the upper body of our H1-2
It is composed of ellipsoid-shaped collision primitives, which are fused together with a smooth transition function.

\subsubsection{Ellipsoid SDFs}

\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:neo:distance:ellipsoid}
  \begin{align}
    k_0 &= \left| \frac{\bm{p} - \bm{c}}{\bm r} \right|_2 \\
    k_1 &= \left| \frac{\bm{p} - \bm{c}}{\bm r^2} \right|_2 \\
    d(\bm{p}) &= \frac{k_0 \cdot (k_0-1)}{k_1}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

\subsubsection{Smooth Transition Function}
\begin{equation}
\text{smin}(d_i,d_j) = d_i+\frac{d_j-d_i}{1-e^{\frac{d_j-d_i}{k\log 2}}} = d_\text{union} \label{eq:limbo:distance:smin}
\end{equation}

\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:neo:dyn}
  \begin{align}
    \bm x(t) &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
      \bm{q} \\ \bm{\dot q}
    \end{bmatrix} \label{eq:neo:dyn:x} \\
    \bm u(t) &=
    \bm{\tau} \label{eq:neo:dyn:u}\\
    \bm{f}_\text{expl}(t) &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
      \bm{\dot q} \\
      \text{cons. FD}(\bm q,\bm{\dot q},\bm{\tau})
    \end{bmatrix} \label{eq:neo:dyn:f}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:neo:constraints}
  \begin{align}
    \min_{\bm x(\cdot),\bm u(\cdot)} \quad & \int_0^{T_f} \left(\omega_q l_\text{q}(\bm x) + \omega_\tau l_\tau(\bm{u}) \right) dt \label{neo:constraints:obj} \\
    \textrm{s.t.} \quad & \qquad \textbf{Dodge Constraint} \nonumber \\
    & d_\text{safe} \leq d(\bm p) & & \forall t \in [0,T_f) \label{eq:neo:constraints:dodge} \\
    & \qquad \textbf{Stability Constraint} \nonumber \\
    & \text{ZMP} \in \gamma \text{PoS} & & \forall t \in [0,T_f) \label{eq:neo:constraints:stability} \\
    & \qquad \textbf{Joint Constraints} \nonumber \\
    & \bm{\underline q} \leq \bm{q} \leq \bm{\overline q} & & \forall t \in [0,T_f) \label{eq:neo:constraints:joint}\\
    & -\bm{\tau}_\text{max} \leq \bm u \leq \bm{\tau}_\text{max} & & \forall t \in [0,T_f)\label{eq:neo:constraints:torque}\\
    & \qquad \textbf{Friction Constraints} \nonumber \\
    & 0 \leq (\mu F_j^z)^2-\|\bm{F}_j\|^2_2 & \forall j \in \{ \text{left}, \text{right} \} \qquad & \forall t \in [0,T_f) \label{eq:neo:constraints:F} \\
    & 0 \leq F_L^z & \forall j \in \{ \text{left}, \text{right} \} \qquad & \forall t \in [0,T_f) \label{eq:neo:constraints:Fz} \\
    & \qquad \textbf{Terminal Pose Constraint} \nonumber \\
    & \begin{bmatrix} \bm q_0-q_\epsilon \\ -\bm v_\text{stat.} \end{bmatrix}
    \leq \bm x(T_f) \leq
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm q_0+q_\epsilon \\ \bm v_\text{stat.} \end{bmatrix} \label{eq:neo:constraints:terminal}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:neo:obj}
  \begin{align}
    l_q(\bm x) &= \frac{\| \bm q-\bm q_0 \|^2_2}{(n_q \cdot \bm{q}_\text{exp}^2)^2}  \label{eq:neo:obj:joint} \\
    l_\tau(\bm u) &= \frac{\|\bm{\tau}\|^2_2}{(n_u \cdot \bm{u}_\text{exp}^2)^2} \label{eq:neo:obj:torque}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
