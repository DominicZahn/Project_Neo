\section{Benchmark Setup} \label{sec:benchmark}

Before we can start developing an optimal approaching object avoidance motion generator, a test setup is required to evaluate the performance of our solution.
In \cref{sec:introduction:comparability} we showed the inconsistency of test setups for a selection of controllers.
These controllers were all learning-based and encapsulate a full pipeline, spawning object detection, tracking, motion generation and execution.
An isolated evaluation of the most important component, the motion generation, is therefore difficult to execute.
Systems like {PAC-MAN} \cite{yang_2026} are tested in real world or simulated environments.
Gazebo \cite{koenig_2004} or Issac-Sim \cite{NVIDIA_Isaac_Sim} are two examples for such environments.
For a focused analysis of collision avoidance motion generation such physic simulations introduce an additional layer of uncertainty by approximating the world with a model.
As our work focuses on the collision avoidance motion we cut out all other components.
The result should be a benchmark that solely relies on the motion generation and a model of the world.
Resulting motions can later be reviewed in real-world or previously mentioned physics simulations.
This would than also include an evaluation of the executing and whole-body controller, something that goes out of the scope of our work.
\par
In the following we will start of by identifying properties necessary for a solid testing method.
\\
\Cref{sec:benchmark:setup} shows how the whole benchmark is constructed from multiple test runs.
The section additionally derives a set of properties a useful test method should have.
Based on these properties we implement during \cref{sec:benchmark:fibonacci,sec:benchmark:thomson} the fundamental steps of the benchmark.
\\
The resulting benchmark method is summarized in \cref{sec:benchmark:algo}.
We also highlight the decisions a user of this testing method must take, as well as parameters, which need to be provided in order to achieve a comparable benchmark.
